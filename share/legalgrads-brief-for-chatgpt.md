**Intro for ChatGPT:** I'm Ryan, founder of Legalgrads. Below is what the business is, the market, the legal hurdles, and our plan. I'm about to talk with Scott, a lawyer friend (he handles traffic tickets in San Diego) who may join me. Please review this and give me advice: what's strong, what's weak, what I'm missing, and what to ask Scott.

**Website:** https://legalgrads.com (attorney signup: https://legalgrads.com/join/)

## What Legalgrads is
A nationwide directory where newly licensed attorneys create their own profiles and get found by small business owners and individuals who can't afford large law firms.
- Tagline: "Hire newly licensed attorneys who are ready to build their practices."
- Lawyers sign up themselves. We verify every bar number with the state bar before a profile goes live.
- Lawyers join free. Later we'd add a flat-fee premium listing that only buys visibility (never per lead or a share of legal fees), then an app.
- Brand: fun, colorful, social and influencer feel, modern sans-serif, graduation cap and tassel. Not a stuffy law-firm look.

## The market
- **Cost keeps small businesses away from lawyers.** In a 2025 Rocket Lawyer survey, 49% of small business owners said they don't use lawyers because they can't afford one. Rocket Lawyer sells legal plans, so this isn't neutral data. (https://www.rocketlawyer.com/binaries/content/assets/us/rocket-lawyer_smb-legal-concerns-a-2025-survey-of-the-small-business-landscape.pdf)
- **Lawyers are expensive.** The average U.S. lawyer charges about $349 an hour, and contract work averages about $373. (https://www.clio.com/resources/legal-trends/compare-lawyer-rates/)
- **New lawyers every year.** 41,713 people were admitted to the bar by exam nationwide in 2025. California's July 2026 results come out Oct 30. (https://thebarexaminer.ncbex.org/2025-statistics/admissions-to-the-bar-by-examination-and-by-transferred-ube-score-2021-2025/)
- **It's hard to get paying clients without a firm.** With no track record, almost no new grads go solo. Only 186 Class of 2025 grads started as solo practitioners, and the biggest firms hired 7.5% fewer new grads. (https://www.nalp.org/uploads/Research/Classof2025_SelectedFindings_5Aug2026.pdf)

## Legal hurdles
- **Referral service rules.** In California, anyone who refers clients to lawyers must be State Bar certified (Bus. & Prof. Code §6155). Jackson v. LegalMatch (2019) applied that to online platforms, so we start as a pure directory with no matching.
- **Fee sharing (ABA Model Rule 5.4).** We never take a cut of legal fees. Avvo shut down its legal services product in 2018 over this.
- **Advertising (Rules 7.2 and 7.3).** No paying for recommendations, and no soliciting clients on lawyers' behalf.
- **Bar verification and malpractice insurance.** Every bar number is checked. In California, an uninsured lawyer must tell clients in writing.
- **Unauthorized practice of law.** We're a platform, not a law firm, and we give no legal advice.
- Rules differ by state, so we'd get an ethics review and expand state by state.

## How we build
- **Now:** a free directory, with verified self-signup live at legalgrads.com/join.
- **Recruit lawyers nationwide:** time pushes to each state's bar results, and work through law school career offices, young-lawyer bar groups, solo-practice incubators, and bar-results communities on Reddit and Facebook. Lawyers get a shareable "I'm on Legalgrads" profile.
- **Reach small businesses:** organic first (plain-English SEO guides like "Do I need a lawyer for an LLC?", social and creator content, small business groups). Add paid ads in the cities with the most lawyer profiles.
- **Later:** flat-fee premium listings, then an app, after an ethics lawyer reviews it.

## Scott's possible role
Owns the legal and ethics side: reviewing attorney terms and site copy and keeping us a directory rather than a referral service. He'd be the trusted attorney face of the brand and would open doors with bar groups and law schools.
